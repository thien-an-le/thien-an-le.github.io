About

I design catalysts that turn ammonia and CO2 into useful energy, and I care about taking them past the paper stage.

From 2012 to 2026 I worked in Korea at KRICT (Korea Research Institute of Chemical Technology), Ajou University and Kyung Hee University, across the full catalyst cycle: synthesis, characterization, kinetic and stability testing, bench-to-pilot scale-up, and technology transfer to industry. Two of those technologies were licensed, to Lotte Chemical Corporation and CES Co., Ltd., and the ammonia-cracking program was selected among Korea's National Top 100 R&D Performances in 2023.

I am now based in Houston, Texas, as a U.S. Permanent Resident. I am looking for R&D roles in heterogeneous catalysis, hydrogen and energy, environmental technology or advanced materials, in industry, national laboratories or academia.

Education: Ph.D. Energy Chemical Engineering, Ajou University (2019) · M.E. Chemical Engineering, Kyung Hee University (2014) · B.E. Chemical Engineering, Industrial University of Ho Chi Minh City (2009). Languages: English (full professional), Vietnamese (native).

How I work

Start from the rate-limiting step

For NH3 cracking on Ru that step is recombinative N2 desorption. Support facets, N doping, La–Ce composites and alkali promoters are each chosen to speed it up, and DFT partners check the mechanism.

Tie structure to performance

Every catalyst gets the same evidence chain: H2-TPR, CO2/NH3-TPD, TPSR, chemisorption, XRD, XPS, HAADF-STEM, Raman, EPR, in situ DRIFTS and synchrotron XAS, read against kinetic data.

Design for the plant, not the paper

Shaped beads instead of powders, low Ru loading, long stability runs and pilot fixed-bed tests. That habit is what carried two catalyst technologies into licensing agreements.

Research & projects

KRICT · 2019–2026

Ru catalysts for low-temperature ammonia cracking

National program "Integrated Original Technology for Use in Hydrogen Production and Storage", Hydrogen & C1 Gas Research Center.

  • Engineered CeO2 supports (CeO2{100} facets, La2O3–CeO2 composites, N-doped CeO2) with Cs promotion.
  • Solvothermal, alcohol-assisted Ru/CeO2 route: 1,319.6 mmol H2 min⁻¹ gRu⁻¹ at 450 °C, stable for 100 h; protected by US 12,251,684 B2.
  • Bench-to-pilot testing and technology transfer to Lotte Chemical Corporation.

Ru-free and shaped catalysts

  • Ni/Al–Ce oxides by one-pot cation–anion double hydrolysis: full NH3 conversion near 550 °C.

  • 3 mm Ru/Al2O3 beads with a K-promoted La–Ce shell: ~80% conversion at ~500 °C for 150 h in a bench reactor.
